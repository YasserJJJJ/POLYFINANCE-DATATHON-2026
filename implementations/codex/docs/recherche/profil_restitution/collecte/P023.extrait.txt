 Qualifying Criteria
 to be defined as a PDA (mandatory)
 The PDA:
     1. Describes the health condition
     2. Explicitly states the decision to be considered
     3. Identifies the target audience (new)
     4. Lists options including if relevant, “wait and see” (e.g., making no change)
     5. Describes positive features of options (benefits)
     6. Describes negative features of options (harms)
     7. Asks patients to think about which positive and negative features of options matter
         most to them OR describes what it is like to experience the consequences of options
         (physical, psychological, social) (revised)

 Essential Criteria
 to reduce the risk of harmful bias (must have)
 The PDA:
     1. Is based on best available evidence that is, where possible, directly applicable to the
         patients and clinicians using it (new)
     2. Describes how potential users were involved in steps of designing, developing and/or
         refining a prototype (new)
     3. Shows negative/positive features of options in a balanced manner (e.g., neutral,
         unbiased, non-directive, complete) (revised)
     4. Reports where the funding came from to develop the PDA and it is clearly stated (e.g.,
         plain language, prominent) (revised)
     5. Provides complete citations to evidence selected
     6. Provides a production or publication date
     7. Provides information about the proposed update policy (or available supporting
         document)
 Additional criteria for screening decisions. PDA:
     8. Describes what the test is supposed to measure
     9. Describes consequences of a positive screening finding that would not have caused
         problems if screening had not been done
     10. Describes possible next steps based on positive and negative test results

 Evaluating           There is evidence that the PDA helps patients:
 Effectiveness        • recognize that a decision needs to be made
                      • know about the available options
                      • know about different features of options
                      • understand that values affect the decision
                      • be clear about which features of options matter most to them
                      • discuss values with their health professionals
                      • become involved in decision making in ways they prefer
                      • improves the match between the features that matter most to the informed
                         patient and the option that is chosen

                      •   If any evaluation of the PDA was conducted, reports the findings with
                          attention to SUNDAE guidelines (Standards for UNiversal reporting of
                          patient Decision Aid Evaluation) (new)
                      •   Describes how evidence of PDA effectiveness was gathered using
                          instruments that have strong psychometric properties (i.e., the evaluation
                          tool is valid and reliable) (new)
